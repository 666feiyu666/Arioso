# Next phase plan

This document states what the next phase must deliver and in what order. It
describes behaviour, not implementation. The implemented snapshot is in
[`current.md`](current.md); the product model is in [`abstract.md`](abstract.md).

## Starting point

- One conversational entry point (MoodMic), a journey from a kept piece to an
  album, a playlist page, and a workflow studio.
- One language-model provider (OpenAI) serves composition, direction, and
  classification.
- One audio provider (Lyria, through the Google Gen AI API).
- A Gemini model judges each MoodMic piece before the listener keeps it.
- Album candidates are generated one at a time.
- `05-playlist` is an album task with references attached, and the ordered list
  inside an album task is also called a playlist.
- Five music types are coded, each with a collection manifest and normalized
  style and standard files. Record counts range from 6 to 606.
- Retrieval records what was retrieved. It does not record what a composer
  adopted, and no stored comparison exists between a grounded and an ungrounded
  composition of the same brief.

## Target outcomes

### 1. Playlist and album are two different objects

- An **album** is generated by Arioso from a confirmed direction: plan, generate,
  review against the plan, keep the candidates that hold together. The listener
  confirms the direction and curates the result.
- A **playlist** is assembled by the listener from pieces that already exist —
  MoodMic pieces, album candidates, exported files. It selects, orders, names,
  plays, and exports. It never generates audio.
- Every place a finished piece appears offers "add to playlist": the MoodMic
  result, the calendar detail, an album candidate, and the playlist page.
  Selecting several pieces creates a playlist in one step.
- A playlist may draw from several albums and several mood conversations. An
  album contains only tracks generated for it.
- Each object has its own identity, page, name, cover, and lifecycle. A playlist
  survives the removal or exclusion of an album it drew from; a piece that no
  longer resolves is reported as missing.
- Reordering, removing, and renaming a playlist involves no regeneration.
- Interface, API, settings, calendar, and documentation use the two words with
  these meanings.

**Done when** a listener builds a playlist from pieces made on different days and
different albums without any generation, and still opens, revises, and exports an
album that Arioso generated.

### 2. DeepSeek can serve every conversational and prompting task

- One setting selects the language-model provider for all tasks: the direction
  conversation, the MoodMic brief, the `MusicSpec` composer, the orchestral
  planner, the album planner and reviewer, the mood classifier, and any prompt
  rewriting.
- With DeepSeek selected and only a DeepSeek key configured, all of those tasks
  complete. No path falls back to OpenAI, and no feature is disabled for want of
  the other provider.
- Settings name the active provider and model and report which credential is
  present, masked.
- Two stored credentials can be switched at any time without restarting the
  server; stored sessions, tasks, and completed work remain available.
- Output structure is provider-neutral: `MusicSpec`, album plans, and orchestral
  plans validate under either provider, and each task records the model that
  answered.
- A missing or invalid DeepSeek credential is reported as a configuration error
  before a task is queued.
- DeepSeek is the default provider for a new installation. OpenAI remains
  selectable.

**Done when** a journey — conversation, brief, album plan, candidate review —
completes on DeepSeek alone, and the existing suite passes with the provider
switched.

### 3. Album and playlist work is faster

- Independent generations run concurrently: album candidates are in flight
  together, and orchestral movements that do not depend on each other do the
  same.
- Concurrency is bounded and configurable through settings.
- Progress is continuous: the interface reports queued, generating, finished,
  and failed counts, and each finished track is playable as it arrives.
- One failed generation is a retryable item, not a failed album. Retrying it
  leaves the other tracks untouched.
- Editing a plan while work is in flight is either refused with a message or
  handled explicitly, and leaves no orphaned or duplicated tracks.
- Leaving the page and returning shows the album as it stands. A server restart
  loses no completed track and duplicates no work.

**Done when** an album at the maximum candidate count completes in a fraction of
its sequential time, and a restart mid-album preserves every finished track.

### 4. ACE-Step 1.5 replaces Lyria as the audio generator

- Every generation — standalone piece, MoodMic piece, album candidate, orchestral
  movement — is produced by ACE-Step 1.5.
- ACE-Step runs as an inference service Arioso reaches over a configured local or
  network endpoint. No Google credential is required for generation.
- Delivered audio keeps its contract: a playable file with a stable URL,
  byte-range playback, a known duration, and the existing task bookkeeping, so
  albums, export, the producer, the calendar, and the journey work unchanged.
- Prompt and duration control map onto what ACE-Step accepts. Where a planned
  duration cannot be honoured, the task records the requested and the actual
  duration.
- A task records which engine produced its audio, and the same plan can be
  rendered by either engine.
- The ACE-Step model or version is a setting. An unreachable service produces a
  retryable error naming the endpoint.
- A side-by-side listening record of one brief under both engines is kept.

**Done when** a MoodMic piece and a full album are generated by ACE-Step with no
Google credential present, and the exported album plays.

### 5. Audio analysis replaces the Gemini listening review

- **Technical analysis.** Each delivered file is measured and reported: silence
  and dropouts, clipping and excessive peaks, abrupt cuts, clicks and impulsive
  defects, hiss, hum and broadband noise, DC offset, loudness and dynamic range.
  Each finding names its position in the track, its severity, and a plain
  description.
- **Noise identification.** Findings distinguish background hiss, hum and mains
  interference, wind and rumble, speech and room leakage, and codec or generation
  artefacts.
- **Deterministic request checks.** Duration against the plan, silence at head
  and tail, channel count and sample rate, and the planned sections in their
  planned order where the plan states them.
- The check runs on every delivered piece, including offline, and over an
  existing library in a batch.
- Audio is not sent off the machine for this check.
- Each piece carries an analysis record with findings, severity, and a summary,
  shown beside the player. The library lists pieces with blocking findings.
- Analysis rejects damaged files and reports measured facts. It does not state a
  musical judgement.
- The Google review model leaves the generation flow, together with its setting,
  its credential, and the `review` pause created for its verdict.

**Done when** a damaged file (clipped, truncated, silent head, injected noise) is
flagged with a locatable finding, a clean generated track passes with no blocking
finding, and no generation calls Gemini.

### 6. The corpus-to-generation relationship is explicit and measured

- Each music type reports its style and standard record counts, the records
  carrying musical evidence, the sources and licences, and the corpus version.
  A type appears in the selector after its collection passes the checks below. An
  unavailable type states its reason.
- Each task states whether it was composed from model knowledge or from retrieved
  evidence, which references were retrieved, which were adopted into the plan,
  and how the query was formed.
- Adoption is recorded separately from retrieval. A plan names the references
  that shaped instrumentation, rhythm, texture, harmony, form, or production, and
  a retrieved reference that did not shape the plan is recorded as unused.
- A fixed brief set is composed twice — with retrieval and without — under the
  same provider, model, and settings. Each pair stores both plans, their
  differences, the retrieval evidence, and a listening record.
- Retrieval ranking is scored separately from audio, on the same brief set:
  whether the expected styles and standards were retrieved, the share of
  irrelevant results, the rate of empty results, and whether an exact title
  lookup matched or reported a gap. A ranking change is accepted on that score.
- The direction stage is evaluated with and without poems on whether the
  Director's description becomes more specific, keeping both outputs. Poetry
  retrieval extends to the workflow studio's direction conversation and is
  evaluated there.
- A new collection is collected, normalized, given a manifest with source and
  licence, and checked for retrievability and for regressions in existing
  queries before the selector offers it.
- Reference titles, scores, and full text stay out of the generation prompt
  unless the listener asks for them.

**Done when** any piece states whether it was grounded and how; each music type
either passes its collection checks or states what is missing; and a stored
comparison exists showing what retrieval changed in the plan and in the heard
result for a fixed brief.

## Implementation order

Each stage leaves the product usable.

### Now

1. **DeepSeek provider.** Every later stage is written in prompts.
2. **Playlist and album separated.** The vocabulary and the two objects are
   assumed by the stages that follow.
3. **Grounding made visible and measurable.** Each task states its grounding and
   what it adopted, and the fixed pair-comparison brief set is recorded. This
   baseline precedes corpus collection and later provider and engine changes.

### Mid-term

4. **Parallel generation.** Depends on albums being the only generating object.
5. **Fully automatic albums.** Depends on generation being fast and bounded: plan,
   generate, review against the plan, keep the coherent candidates, and present a
   sequenced album for acceptance, resequencing, or use in a playlist.
6. **Corpus expansion.** Complete and validate the four other music types and
   extend the poetry corpus through the checked pipeline, then re-run the pair set
   over the enlarged corpus.

### Long-term

7. **Audio analysis and noise identification.** The measurement layer is built and
   shown read-only during the mid-term; it becomes the delivery check here.
8. **ACE-Step 1.5.** Replaces the component the rest of the system assumes.
   Analysis thresholds are calibrated on ACE-Step output.
9. **The grounded-versus-ungrounded verdict.** With provider, engine, and analysis
   settled, the brief set is run against the final stack and the result is
   reported, including pairs where retrieval produced no audible difference.

## Boundaries

- No general-purpose audio editor. Playlists sequence existing pieces.
- No second agent platform. The provider switch changes which model answers.
- Analysis reports measurements. Musical judgement stays with the listener.
- Which model composed a plan and which engine rendered a track are always
  recorded and shown.
- A collection is kept or removed on a recorded retrieval and comparison result.
- No corpus text, title, or score in a generation prompt without the listener
  asking for it.

## Open questions

- Whether a playlist can contain an album as a whole, or only its tracks.
- Which of ACE-Step's conditioning options are exposed to the listener.
- Which analysis findings block delivery and which warn.
- Whether the workflow studio keeps a separate theme-album workflow.
- Whether pair comparison is judged by the listener, a scored rubric, or a
  language model describing the difference between the two plans.
- Whether the corpus grows by named music type or by mood and atmosphere.
- How many briefs a comparison set needs, and whether it re-runs on every corpus
  change or on a release.
