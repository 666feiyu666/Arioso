Create the first movement of an original three-movement orchestral symphonic poem set in a mysterious nineteenth-century opera house. The work may evoke gothic theatrical romance at a high level, but it must not quote, reproduce, or closely imitate any identifiable melody, harmony sequence, arrangement, or recording from an existing musical or stage work.

Shared musical contract for all three movements:

- Purely instrumental. No singing, no choir, no spoken words, and no vocal sounds.
- A full late-Romantic symphony orchestra: rich strings, piccolo and woodwinds, four horns, trumpets, trombones, tuba, harp, celesta, timpani, bass drum, cymbals, and tubular bells. A pipe organ may reinforce major structural moments but must remain integrated into the orchestral texture rather than replacing the orchestra.
- The original five-note identity motif has scale-degree contour 1–4–flat-6–5–2, with the rhythm long–short–long–short–long. Develop this motif through fragmentation, augmentation, inversion, orchestral transfer, and harmonic reinterpretation; do not introduce an unrelated principal theme.
- The principal tonal world is E minor with occasional Phrygian F-natural color and chromatic diminished tension. The central relaxed movement may open into the relative-major region, but the motif and orchestral identity must remain recognizable.
- A deep, grand opera-house acoustic with natural orchestral dynamics, clear instrumental depth, dark lower-register weight, and no electronic instruments or modern cinematic sound-design effects.

Movement I — Shadow overture, approximately two to two-and-a-half minutes:

Begin almost inaudibly with divided low strings, contrabassoon, and soft timpani on an open E and B. Reveal fragments of the identity motif in bass clarinet and stopped horns before allowing the full five-note form to appear in cellos. Establish a restless 6/8 pulse around 84 BPM.

Build tension in waves rather than moving immediately to maximum volume. Let violins and woodwinds pass fragments across the opera-house space while low brass and timpani interrupt with shortened versions of the motif. Introduce harp and celesta as cold points of light against the dark orchestra.

Reach one controlled orchestral climax in which brass, strings, percussion, tubular bells, and pipe organ state the motif together. This is the first dramatic summit, not the final climax of the complete work.

After the climax, withdraw gradually. Remove organ, heavy brass, and percussion. Let solo violin and clarinet soften the motif into longer legato phrases while the harmony turns toward G major without fully arriving. End quietly on a suspended, warm B-centered sonority with harp resonance and high strings, unresolved but no longer threatening, so a relaxed second movement can begin naturally.
