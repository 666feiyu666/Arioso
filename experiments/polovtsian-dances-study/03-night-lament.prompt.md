Create the third movement of an original long-form orchestral symphonic poem. It follows a separately generated orchestral dance that ended with a decisive D-minor cadence. Do not quote, reproduce, or closely imitate any existing composition or recording.

Shared musical contract for the complete work:

- A dramatic late-Romantic orchestral world suggesting a vast steppe, distant ritual gathering, communal dance, and the solitude that follows.
- D minor with Phrygian color, especially E-flat leaning toward D. Preserve the established tonal world even while the tempo and meter become more reflective.
- The recurring original identity motif is a rising minor second, then a leap upward by a perfect fourth, followed by a stepwise descent. In this movement, transform it through augmentation, inversion, fragmented echoes, and changes of register without introducing an unrelated principal theme.
- The same symphonic orchestra and concert hall: dark lower strings, lyrical violins, oboe, clarinet, bassoon, four horns, restrained trumpets and trombones, timpani, bass drum, cymbals, and hand drums. Wordless mixed chorus may appear only as a distant orchestral color. No modern electronic instruments.
- Preserve the same natural orchestral perspective and dynamic scale as the earlier movements.

Movement III — Night lament and remembrance, approximately two to two-and-a-half minutes:

Begin only after a brief natural breath following the previous movement's final chord. Establish a slow 6/8 pulse around 64 BPM. Solo cello recalls the identity motif in augmentation over divided low strings, followed by an oboe answer that turns the motif downward. Keep percussion absent at first.

Develop a restrained nocturne. Let clarinet, bassoon, solo violin, and cello exchange incomplete memories of the motif while muted horns sustain distant D-minor and E-flat colors. The wordless chorus may enter almost imperceptibly on long vowels, blended behind the strings rather than functioning as a vocal lead.

In the middle of the movement, allow a faint memory of the earlier dance rhythm to appear in pizzicato basses and very soft hand drum, but do not restore the full dance. Increase harmonic tension and orchestral breadth briefly, then withdraw again.

During the final thirty seconds, reduce the music to tremolo violas, low strings, solo oboe, and quiet timpani. Move from D minor toward an unresolved open A pedal. End softly on that sustained dominant tension, with a final incomplete fragment of the identity motif and natural hall resonance, preparing an immediate final movement rather than providing closure.
