Create the first movement of an original long-form orchestral dance suite. The complete work will continue in a separately generated second movement. Do not quote, reproduce, or closely imitate any existing composition or recording.

Shared musical contract for the complete work:

- A dramatic late-Romantic orchestral dance sound suggesting a vast steppe, a distant ritual gathering, and mounting communal energy.
- D minor with occasional Phrygian color, especially E-flat leaning toward D. Broad 4/4 pulse around 96 BPM, with brief rubato only in the opening.
- A recurring original identity motif: a rising minor second, then a leap upward by a perfect fourth, followed by a stepwise descent. Present it in different registers without replacing it with an unrelated theme.
- Full symphonic orchestra: dark lower strings, lyrical violins, oboe, clarinet, bassoon, four horns, restrained trumpets and trombones, timpani, bass drum, cymbals, and hand drums. Wordless mixed chorus may appear only as a distant orchestral color.
- Deep concert-hall perspective, natural orchestral dynamics, coherent acoustic space, and no modern electronic instruments.

Movement I — Introduction and distant procession, approximately two to two-and-a-half minutes:

Begin in near-darkness with low strings holding an open D and A, isolated bassoon phrases, and distant horn responses. Introduce the identity motif slowly in solo oboe, leaving large spaces between phrases. Gradually establish the 96 BPM pulse through muted hand drum and pizzicato low strings.

Develop into a restrained procession rather than a climax. Pass fragments of the motif from woodwinds to violins while horns broaden the harmony. Allow the wordless chorus to enter very softly behind the orchestra, without text and without becoming the main subject.

During the final thirty to forty seconds, deliberately reduce the orchestration and energy. Remove brass and chorus first, then thin the upper strings. Leave a quiet D pedal in cellos and basses, soft timpani heartbeat, tremolo violas, and one incomplete oboe statement of the motif. End quietly and openly, without a grand cadence, so the next movement can begin from the same atmosphere.
