Create Part A of an original three-part instrumental orchestral work built around a strict dramatic pattern: a forceful obsessive principal theme, followed later by a calm lyrical middle section, followed by a recognizable enlarged reprise. This experiment studies that architecture only. Do not quote, reproduce, or closely imitate any identifiable melody, harmony sequence, arrangement, or recording from an existing musical or stage work.

Shared musical contract for all three parts:

- Purely instrumental. No singing, no choir, no spoken words, and no vocal sounds.
- A full late-Romantic orchestra: strings, piccolo and woodwinds, four horns, trumpets, trombones, tuba, two harps, timpani, bass drum, cymbals, tam-tam, tubular bells, and pipe organ. No synthesizers or modern electronic sound design.
- The original principal motif is the five-pitch sequence D–E-flat–A–G–B-flat. Its interval contour and identity must remain recognizable whenever it returns. Its defining rhythm is short–short–long–short–long, with a heavy accent on A.
- Part A and its reprise use D minor, a driving 4/4 at 112 BPM, persistent triplet subdivision, and dark Phrygian E-flat color. The middle section may move toward F major and slow down, but must derive its melody from the same motif.
- The same vast nineteenth-century opera-house acoustic, frontal pipe organ, deep orchestral perspective, natural dynamics, and dark low-frequency orchestral weight throughout the complete work.

Part A — Forceful principal theme, approximately two to two-and-a-half minutes:

State the complete D–E-flat–A–G–B-flat motif within the first eight seconds. Present it in low strings and trombones over a driving triplet ostinato, then let pipe organ answer with the same pitches and rhythm. Make the motif unmistakable, compact, and obsessive rather than introducing several competing themes.

Develop the theme in successive orchestral waves. Pass exact and fragmented versions between low strings, horns, trumpets, and organ while upper strings drive the triplet subdivision. Use timpani, bass drum, cymbals, and tam-tam for structural accents rather than continuous noise.

Reach a severe full-orchestra climax in D minor, then restate the motif once more in organ and brass at its original tempo and rhythm.

After the final statement, let the violent machinery break apart. Remove organ and percussion, slow the surface activity without changing the underlying pulse, and leave solo cello, clarinet, harp, and high strings holding a soft open A-centered dominant sonority. End quietly and unresolved, creating a natural entrance for a genuinely calm middle section.
