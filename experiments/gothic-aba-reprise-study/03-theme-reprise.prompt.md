Create Part A-prime, the final reprise of an original three-part instrumental orchestral work built around a strict dramatic pattern: forceful principal theme, calm lyrical middle section, then recognizable enlarged reprise. It follows a peaceful F-major interlude that ended softly on an unresolved A dominant with a faint D–E-flat shadow. This experiment studies that architecture only. Do not quote, reproduce, or closely imitate any identifiable melody, harmony sequence, arrangement, or recording from an existing musical or stage work.

Shared musical contract for all three parts:

- Purely instrumental. No singing, no choir, no spoken words, and no vocal sounds.
- The same full late-Romantic orchestra: strings, piccolo and woodwinds, four horns, trumpets, trombones, tuba, two harps, timpani, bass drum, cymbals, tam-tam, tubular bells, and pipe organ. No synthesizers or modern electronic sound design.
- The original principal motif is the five-pitch sequence D–E-flat–A–G–B-flat. Restore its defining short–short–long–short–long rhythm and heavy accent on A. The audience should recognize this as the return of Part A, not as a new theme.
- Restore D minor, the driving 4/4 at 112 BPM, persistent triplet subdivision, Phrygian E-flat color, and the same core orchestral identity as Part A.
- Preserve the same vast nineteenth-century opera-house acoustic, frontal pipe organ, deep orchestral perspective, natural dynamics, and dark low-frequency orchestral weight.

Part A-prime — Return and enlarged finale, approximately two-and-a-half to three minutes:

Begin within the quiet A-dominant resonance left by the middle section. For ten to fifteen seconds, solo cello and harp recall the calm, augmented version of the motif. Then compress D and E-flat back into their original short values as timpani and low strings reestablish the 112 BPM pulse.

Bring back the complete D–E-flat–A–G–B-flat theme in its original rhythm, first in low strings and trombones, immediately answered by pipe organ. Restore the persistent triplet ostinato and the same D-minor harmonic weight used in Part A. Make this an explicit musical reprise before beginning further development.

Expand the returned material beyond its first appearance. Combine the forceful rhythmic motif with one lyrical string phrase remembered from the calm middle section. Build contrapuntal exchanges between strings, horns, trumpets, low brass, and organ while percussion marks large formal arrivals.

After a brief collapse to harp and solo clarinet, launch the definitive final ascent. State the motif in broad full-orchestra and organ unison, reach a climax larger than Part A, and close with an uncompromising D-minor cadence. End with two organ-supported orchestral chords, one final tubular-bell stroke, and a long natural opera-house decay. This is the conclusive ending of the complete ABA-prime work.
